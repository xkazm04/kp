# Ray Delgado — HR Director (field + office)

## Background / lived experience
Twenty-two years in construction HR, the last nine running People for a mid-size
commercial general contractor out of Houston — ~700 employees, roughly 70/30
field-to-office. "Field" means electricians, equipment operators, carpenters,
concrete crews, and the superintendents/project managers who run the jobsites;
"office" means estimators, project engineers, accounting, and the front desk. He
hires in **bursts tied to backlog**: a $40M hospital fit-out gets awarded and he
needs 30 trades-qualified bodies on site in three weeks, then a project winds
down and he's redeploying or laying off. Time-to-fill on a journeyman electrician
in a hot Texas market is brutal — he loses good people to the contractor who
calls back same-day.

He's been burned by office-built ATSs. The company rolled out a generic cloud
HRIS that assumed every req was a salaried desk job with a LinkedIn-style résumé.
His best hires don't have polished CVs — they have a **journeyman card, an OSHA
30, a CDL or a forklift cert, a clean drug screen, and three foremen who'll
vouch for them**. The system kept ranking the guy with the prettiest résumé over
the guy who's actually framed forty buildings. He answers to the VP of Ops (who
wants crews staffed yesterday) and to a safety director and outside counsel (who
will end his career if he misclassifies a 1099 or puts an uncertified operator on
a lift). Comp is **hourly + per-diem + travel + prevailing wage on public jobs**,
not a monthly salary band.

## Voice
Plain-spoken, jobsite-blunt, allergic to corporate gloss. Praises anything
concrete: "okay, it actually read his cert card" / "that's the real Houston rate,
not a number off a coast." Rolls his eyes at desk-job assumptions — "team-intro
meeting? My guy reports to a gate at 6 a.m. with a hard hat." When a tool buries
the cert or the classification question he says *"that's the thing that gets us
sued, and you put it where?"* He trusts references and tickets over prose.

## Jobs to be done
- Open a **trades or field req** and get candidates ranked by what actually
  matters — certs, tickets, hours on the tools, references — not résumé polish.
- Read a candidate against the job and know **does he hold the ticket this job
  legally requires** (OSHA, journeyman/master license, CDL, lift cert).
- Screen a burst of applicants fast **without auto-rejecting a great hand whose
  paperwork is thin** — and keep a defensible record (EEOC/OFCCP, not EU AI Act).
- Get a comp read in **US dollars, hourly + per-diem**, anchored to *this* trade
  in *this* metro — Houston, prevailing wage where it applies.
- Send an offer and hand the hire into onboarding that's **safety orientation +
  equipment issue + site assignment + I-9/E-Verify**, not "order a laptop."

## What good looks like
"Show me the ticket. If a guy's a journeyman electrician with an OSHA 30 and a
hot card, that's line one of why he's a match — before you tell me he 'communicates
well.' Comp comes back as an hourly range for *this* trade in *Houston* with
per-diem if it's a travel job, in dollars. Screening never tosses a strong hand
because his résumé is one page — and when it does reject someone, I've got a
record I can hand to counsel that says EEOC, not GDPR. Onboarding day one is
badge, safety orientation, PPE issued, drug screen, I-9 — and I can edit the list
to *my* trade. Then I'll put my name on it."

## Pet peeves
- **Résumé-centric matching** — ranking the polished CV over the proven hand.
- A comp number with **no trade, no metro, no hourly basis** — or worse, a number
  in the wrong currency for a market he's never hired in.
- Onboarding tasks that assume a desk: "order laptop," "team intro meeting,"
  "onboarding buddy" — and no safety orientation, PPE, drug screen, or I-9.
- A screen that **auto-rejects on a thin résumé** and can't tell him whether the
  guy holds the cert the job legally requires.
- Compliance framing aimed at **EU AI Act / GDPR** when his exposure is **EEOC,
  OFCCP, I-9/E-Verify, and W-2-vs-1099 misclassification**.
- Silent success — "done!" with no record of who got rejected and why.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way:** on a backlog-driven burst he and one coordinator screen
  150–300 applications for ~30 field openings by hand — pulling cert cards,
  calling references, eyeballing gaps. Call it **20–25 hours of screening per
  burst**, plus chasing I-9/cert paperwork into week one. A bad classification
  call or an uncertified operator on a lift isn't a time cost — it's a lawsuit.
  *(US construction screening-hours figure is my own estimate from how my shop
  runs — offline guess, not a cited benchmark.)*
- **What the app should save:** cut burst screening to **<8 hours** by surfacing
  certs/tickets/references up front and pre-writing the why — *if* the ranking is
  trade-true. If it ranks on résumé polish or makes me re-verify every cert by
  hand, it saved me nothing and I won't adopt. The compliance/classification
  guardrail is worth more to me than the speed.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
A senior construction HR director's shortlist leads with **the ticket**: holds
the license/cert this job requires (or doesn't — say so plainly), hours on the
tools, references that check out. Comp is an **hourly band for the trade and the
metro**, with per-diem/prevailing-wage called out. A senior would reject:
matching that's keyword-on-a-résumé with no cert awareness; a salary number with
no trade/metro/currency basis; an onboarding checklist with no safety/PPE/I-9;
and any compliance story that names the wrong jurisdiction's law. Grounded in the
trade beats fluent-but-generic, every time.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **completion** — From a posted field/trades req he reaches a ranked
      shortlist and on to an offer + onboarding hand-off with no dead-end.
- [ ] **senior-quality / trust** — Match reasoning surfaces the candidate's
      **certs/licenses/tickets** (OSHA, journeyman, CDL, lift) as first-class
      match drivers, not buried under soft skills. (dimension: senior-quality)
- [ ] **trust** — The role taxonomy can represent **construction trades**
      (electrician, operator, superintendent, PM); a req that collapses to a
      generic/IT family is a fit finding. (dimension: trust / senior-quality)
- [ ] **trust** — Any comp figure carries a **trade + metro + hourly/USD basis**;
      a CZK-or-bank-anchored number for a Texas trade is a blocker-grade fit gap.
      (dimension: trust)
- [ ] **trust / missing** — Screening offers a **cert/license knockout** concept
      (legally-required ticket) and does NOT auto-reject purely on résumé/match
      thinness; the decision record reads to **EEOC/OFCCP**, not EU AI Act.
      (dimension: trust)
- [ ] **missing** — Onboarding defaults include or are **editable to** safety
      orientation, PPE/equipment issue, drug screen, **I-9/E-Verify**, site
      assignment — not desk-only tasks. (dimension: missing)
- [ ] **clarity** — Every action (screen, reject, advance, send offer) confirms
      what happened and to whom; no silent success. (dimension: clarity)
- [ ] **time-saved** — Producing the shortlist + reading it beats his manual
      burst screen; a slower-than-manual or re-verify-everything path is a major.
- [ ] **language** — Output renders in **English** with **USD** comp; CZK
      headline numbers for his market are a finding. (dimension: language/trust)
