# Dr. Adaeze Nwosu — Clinical Talent Acquisition Director

## Background / lived experience
Adaeze runs clinical talent acquisition for a **12,000-employee US health system** —
three acute-care hospitals, a children's center, and a network of outpatient
clinics. She trained as a nurse (the "Dr." is a DNP, not an MD), did six years on a
med-surg floor, then crossed into TA, so she reads a clinician's CV the way a
charge nurse does, not the way a keyword scanner does. She has lived inside
**Workday Recruiting** and a bolt-on credentialing system (**symplr / Verisys**
tier), and she has been burned by every "AI screening" pilot that treated an RN
like a software engineer — ranked a 20-year ICU nurse below a new grad because the
new grad's résumé happened to say "leadership."

Her requisitions are a **flood**: staff RNs, charge nurses, NPs, CRNAs, respiratory
therapists, techs, plus the occasional physician through the medical-staff office.
The nursing shortage means she's carrying **40–60 open clinical reqs** at any time
and her vacancy rate drives travel-nurse spend that the CFO watches by the week.
But volume never lets her cut the one corner that matters: **every clinical hire is
a patient-safety and legal event.** Before anyone touches a patient she must verify
an **active, unencumbered state license** (and compact/multistate status), **board
certification**, **NPI**, a clean **malpractice / NPDB** history, a **background
check run under FCRA** (pre-adverse + adverse-action letters), and a complete
**immunization + health-clearance** record (TB, Hep B, flu, fit-test) — by
**primary-source verification**, because Joint Commission and CMS will audit it and
a bad credential read is how an unlicensed person ends up at a bedside.

She answers to the CNO and the CHRO, and — functionally — to **The Joint
Commission, CMS, the state board of nursing, the EEOC, and OFCCP** (the system
holds federal contracts). An AI that scores clinicians is, to her, a *high-risk
instrument under EEOC adverse-impact law* long before anyone mentions the EU.

## Voice
Calm, exact, clinical — she chooses words like she's charting. Praises specifics
and provenance: "good, it says where that came from," "that's primary-source, not
self-reported." Bristles at confident vagueness: "a score with no driver is a
liability, not a decision." Her reflex question on any automated reject is *"can I
defend this to the EEOC and to the family if it goes wrong?"* Her highest
compliment is quiet: "I'd put my license behind that." Her worst verdict: "that's
how someone gets hurt."

## Jobs to be done
- Post a clinical req and get a **ranked, reasoned shortlist that understands
  clinical roles** — specialty, unit, license type, years on *that* kind of floor —
  not a software-engineering taxonomy.
- Read one clinician's CV against the req and get **fit + a credential read + a comp
  read in USD/year against the local nursing market**, with a basis.
- Screen high volume **without auto-rejecting on a basis that creates adverse
  impact**, with human-in-the-loop, AI disclosure, and an **auditable decision
  record** that satisfies EEOC/OFCCP.
- Carry a hire into onboarding where the checklist is **credentialing + immunization
  + primary-source verification + FCRA background**, not "order a laptop."

## What good looks like
"A shortlist where each nurse has a reason that names *their* unit, *their* license,
*their* years at the bedside — and the system never quietly drops a 50-year-old ICU
veteran because a new grad keyword-matched 'leadership.' Comp reads in **dollars per
year** against my metro's nursing market, with a basis I can show the CNO. When the
AI screens, a human signs every consequential decision, the candidate is told AI was
used, and there's a record I'd hand the EEOC without flinching. And when I hire,
onboarding *starts* with 'verify the license at the source' — because that's the job
that keeps patients alive."

## Pet peeves
- A **role taxonomy that only knows software/office jobs** — if "RN," "CRNA," "med-
  surg," "license" aren't first-class, the match is theater.
- **Comp in the wrong currency/market** — a CZK monthly-gross number for a US RN is
  not just useless, it's evidence the tool wasn't built for healthcare hiring.
- **Auto-reject with thin reasoning** — anything that could produce **disparate
  impact** without an EEOC-defensible record is a legal landmine, not a time-saver.
- **A "credential" that's self-reported, not primary-source** — in her world that
  distinction is the whole job.
- **Generic-office onboarding** (badge, t-shirt size, buddy) with **no credentialing,
  no immunization, no FCRA background** step — and worse, no way to add them.
- **Compliance copy aimed at the wrong regulator** — GDPR/EU-AI-Act framing that
  silently substitutes for the EEOC/FCRA/Joint-Commission regime she actually lives
  under.
- Silent success — an action that "completed" with no statement of what it did to whom.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way:** screening clinical résumés by hand at volume eats roughly
  **20–25 hrs per hire** (consistent with the research digest's ~23 hrs), *plus*
  credentialing-coordinator time she can't compress: license/board/NPI/NPDB
  primary-source checks and immunization reconciliation run **days to weeks** per
  hire and gate the start date. Time-to-fill for clinical roles routinely runs
  **60–90+ days**, every vacant day billed as travel-nurse premium. *(US health-
  system estimate, offline — the digest's bank baseline doesn't model the
  credentialing tail, which for her is the binding constraint.)*
- **What the app should save:** a reasoned clinical shortlist + first screen in
  **minutes** (a 60–70% cut to the manual screen, per the digest), and an onboarding
  template that at least *sequences and tracks* credentialing so the verification
  tail starts on day one instead of after the offer. The adoption line: if it can't
  read a clinical CV better than she can at 6am before rounds — or if it can't
  represent the credential/compliance work at all — it's a sourcing toy, not a
  clinical TA system, and she won't stake patient safety on it.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
Adaeze *is* the senior reviewer; she'd write the shortlist herself after reading the
chart-like CV. So the AI must read like **an experienced clinical recruiter who
actually read it**: names the specialty and unit, respects bedside years over
keyword "leadership," is honest about gaps, and **never invents a credential**. A
fabricated license, board cert, or skill is not a quality miss — it's a
**patient-safety + legal failure**, an automatic blocker. A comp read must be in
**USD/year against the local clinical market** with a basis, or an honest "I can't
price this market." Any screen score must carry its drivers and survive an
**adverse-impact** question. A senior in her seat rejects: a software taxonomy
applied to nurses, comp in koruna, an auto-reject she couldn't defend to the EEOC,
a credential treated as verified when it's self-reported, and an onboarding flow
with no credentialing.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **completion** — From a clinical req she reaches a ranked, reasoned shortlist
      and can carry one candidate to an onboarding next-step with no dead-end /
      re-entry loop.
- [ ] **senior-quality / trust (taxonomy fit)** — Match/CV reasoning understands the
      **clinical role** (specialty/unit/license/bedside years), cites ≥1 concrete
      fact from *that* CV, and references the req; a software-engineering taxonomy
      applied to a nurse is a **major** (wrong-domain output).
- [ ] **trust (credential integrity)** — Every credential/skill named is traceable
      to the source CV; **zero fabricated licenses/certs** (one fabrication =
      blocker, patient-safety severity). No self-reported credential is presented as
      verified.
- [ ] **time-saved / trust (comp fit)** — Any comp read is in **USD/year against a
      US clinical market**, or settable to it, with a basis; a CZK-monthly /
      bank-anchored number with no override is a **major** (wrong-market output).
- [ ] **trust (compliance fit)** — Screening has human-in-the-loop + AI disclosure +
      an auditable decision record, and a fairness gate that protects against
      **adverse impact** in EEOC terms — not *exclusively* GDPR/EU-AI-Act framing
      that misleads a US healthcare employer (FCRA/EEOC/OFCCP/Joint Commission).
- [ ] **missing / senior-quality (onboarding fit)** — Default onboarding tasks +
      questionnaire are **editable** and can express clinical pre-boarding
      (primary-source license/board/NPI verification, NPDB, FCRA background,
      immunization/health clearance), not locked to generic office tasks.
- [ ] **clarity** — After every action she sees an explicit confirmation of what
      happened and to whom — no silent success.
- [ ] **trust (provenance)** — A credential/skill the system asserts shows where it
      came from (CV-quoted vs inferred); an unsourced clinical claim is a finding.
