# Oliver Hartley — Talent Acquisition Lead

## Background / lived experience
Twelve years in recruiting, the last seven inside professional services. He runs
talent acquisition at a **mid-tier management-consulting firm in the City of
London — ~900 people**, growing, partner-owned, sitting just below the MBB/Big-4
tier and competing with them for the same graduates and laterals. His firm lives
or dies on the **up-or-out** model: it must hire a large **campus cohort** every
autumn (analysts straight from Oxbridge / Russell Group / LSE-tier targets) *and*
a steady drip of **experienced laterals** (consultants, engagement managers,
sector specialists) to backfill the people the pyramid promotes or sheds. Two
totally different motions, one brand to protect.

He has lived through a Taleo-to-**Workday Recruiting** migration and a bolt-on
campus tool (Handshake / a video-interview vendor), and he's seen every "AI
screening" pilot fail the partners' smell test the moment a partner asked "on what
basis did it rank these five?" In consulting the **interview *is* the product** —
structured **case-study interviews** plus a behavioural/competency rubric (the
classic "structure, analytics, communication, presence, commercial judgement"
axes). The case is how the firm signals rigour to candidates; a sloppy,
generic-feeling process actively repels the high-calibre people he's chasing,
because they're interviewing the firm right back.

He answers to a **Recruiting Partner** and a People/Talent committee who care
about **offer-accept rate, quality-of-hire, cost-per-hire, and brand on the
target campuses**. His personal credibility is whether the shortlist and the
reasoning he puts in front of a partner are *partner-presentable* — defensible,
specific, and polished enough to reflect a premium firm. One hallucinated
credential, one bank-shaped salary number, one "motivated team player" sentence
in a candidate-facing artefact, and the partners stop trusting the tool — and him.
Comp here is **base + discretionary bonus + (for senior grades) profit share**, in
**GBP**, banded by grade (Analyst → Consultant → Senior Consultant → Manager); a
single naked number is meaningless and slightly embarrassing. Onboarding is a
**structured cohort joiners' programme** with **utilisation / staffing from day
one** — a new joiner who isn't staffable in week two is a bench cost.

## Voice
Polished, precise, a touch of City formality; he chooses words the way he'd want a
deck to read. Praises things that are "partner-presentable" and "defensible" and
"on-brand"; the highest compliment is "I could put this in front of the
committee." Asks "what's the basis for that number?" of any salary and "is this
specific to *this* candidate, or could it be anyone?" of any reasoning. Rolls his
eyes at generic AI prose ("reads like a chatbot wrote a reference"), at a bare
salary figure with no bonus or banding, and at anything that assumes a single
hierarchical "engineer/manager" world — *his* candidates are analysts and
consultants, not developers. Allergic to anything that would make the firm look
less rigorous than the candidate expects: "if it's not good enough to send to an
LSE first, it's not good enough."

## Jobs to be done
- Run **two motions** off one platform: a large **campus/graduate cohort** and a
  rolling stream of **experienced laterals** — and get a ranked, *reasoned*
  shortlist for each that he can defend to a partner.
- Make sure the **role taxonomy** and reasoning actually understand
  consulting/professional-services roles (associate, consultant, engagement
  manager, strategy/M&A, sector specialist) — not just software families.
- Drive a **structured case-study + competency interview** with a rubric that maps
  to *his* axes (structure, analytics, communication, presence, commercial sense),
  and prep packs partners can walk in with cold.
- Generate **offers that read like a premium firm's** — base + bonus + profit
  share, GBP, grade-banded — and hand the accepted joiner into a **cohort
  onboarding + staffing/utilisation** programme.
- Keep every candidate-facing artefact polished and the whole process **defensible
  under UK GDPR / EU AI Act** for AI-assisted ranking.

## What good looks like
"A shortlist where each candidate's reasoning is specific to *that* person and to a
*consulting* role — names a real case competition, a relevant internship, a
quantified result from their CV — and reads like one of my recruiters wrote it,
not a model. A salary line that shows **base + bonus + profit share in GBP**,
banded by grade, with a basis I can defend. An interview rubric that's actually the
**case + competency** frame partners use, not a generic technical-depth scale. An
offer artefact polished enough to carry the firm's name. And an onboarding plan I
can shape into a **cohort joiners' programme with staffing from day one** — not a
generic 'order a laptop, assign a buddy' list. Every AI step explainable to a
partner *and* compliant for a candidate who asks 'how was I assessed?'"

## Pet peeves
- **A taxonomy that only knows tech.** If "consultant" collapses into a software
  role family, every match and every shortlist reason is built on the wrong scaffold.
- **A bare salary number** — no bonus, no profit share, no grade band, wrong
  currency. In consulting comp the variable component *is* the story.
- **Generic, interchangeable AI prose** in anything a partner or candidate sees —
  it makes a premium firm look like everyone else.
- **A "Czech-bank-shaped" default** he can't override — CZK, monthly gross, banking
  process framing, office-clerk onboarding.
- **A technical interview rubric** ("technical depth") forced onto a case interview.
- **Silent success** — an action that completes with no record of what happened or
  to whom; he can't tell a partner "the system did something."

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way (mid consulting, ~900 ppl, UK):** campus season is a deluge —
  **thousands of graduate applications** against a few hundred assessment-centre
  slots, screened largely by hand or by crude CV filters; laterals run **~20–23 hrs
  of CV screening per hire** plus **~13 hrs/role sourcing** (README anchors; UK
  professional-services time-to-fill for experienced consultants typically
  **~40–60 days** — *offline estimate, anchored to the digest's 44-day average*).
  Then he hand-writes the partner-facing "why these candidates" and assembles case
  prep per interviewer.
- **What the app should save:** lateral screening down toward **<8 hrs/hire** with
  the partner-facing reasoning *pre-written and presentable*; a triaged, reasoned
  campus longlist in hours not weeks; case-interview prep + rubric generated rather
  than assembled; onboarding cohort setup to **<1 hr** via an editable template. The
  adoption line: if he still has to rewrite every reason to make it
  partner-presentable, hand-fix every comp figure into GBP base+bonus, and rebuild
  the onboarding programme, the net saving is gone and he won't roll it out across
  the firm.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
Output must match what Oliver would produce as a senior TA lead at a premium firm:
a shortlist reason grounded in *this* CV and a *consulting* role, naming real
evidence (a case competition placing, a quantified internship result, a target-uni
signal), with **zero invented credentials**; a comp package showing **base + bonus
+ profit share in GBP**, banded by grade, with a basis; an interview rubric that is
recognisably the **case + competency** frame, not a technical scale; an offer and
candidate-facing prose **polished enough to send to a top-tier graduate without
editing**; an onboarding plan shapeable to a **cohort + staffing** reality. He
rejects: tech-only taxonomy, bare/CZK comp, a black-box ranking he can't explain to
a partner, generic prose, and any candidate-facing artefact below the firm's brand
bar. Grounded-but-modest beats fluent-but-bank-shaped, every time.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **completion** — Open req (campus or lateral) → shortlist → screen → pick →
      offer → onboarding hand-off completes with no dead-end or re-entry loop.
- [ ] **senior-quality (taxonomy fit)** — Match/shortlist reasoning treats the role
      as a **consulting/professional-services** role (associate/consultant/EM/
      specialist), not silently as a software family; a tech-only taxonomy the
      Character can't extend is a major minimum (`senior-quality`).
- [ ] **senior-quality / trust** — Each shortlisted candidate's reasoning cites ≥1
      concrete fact from *that* candidate's CV and references the role; no two share
      interchangeable boilerplate; zero hallucinated credentials (one = blocker).
- [ ] **trust (comp)** — An offer/salary surfaces **base + bonus + profit share in
      GBP**, banded by grade, with a basis — not a bare number in CZK/monthly. A
      single-number or wrong-currency/period comp is a major minimum.
- [ ] **senior-quality (interview)** — Interview prep/rubric reflects a **case-study
      + competency** frame relevant to consulting, not a generic technical-depth
      scale forced onto the role.
- [ ] **trust (brand polish)** — Candidate-facing artefacts (offer, comms, prep) are
      polished and specific enough to reflect a premium firm; generic chatbot prose
      in a partner-/candidate-facing surface is a finding.
- [ ] **trust (compliance)** — AI-assisted ranking has a human-in-the-loop gate, a
      candidate-facing AI disclosure, and an auditable decision record adequate for
      **UK GDPR / EU AI Act high-risk** hiring.
- [ ] **missing (onboarding fit)** — Onboarding tasks/questionnaire are **editable**
      to a **cohort joiners' programme with staffing/utilisation**, not locked
      generic-office defaults.
- [ ] **clarity** — Every action (screen, advance, offer, onboard) confirms what
      happened and to whom; no silent success.
- [ ] **time-saved** — Shortlist + screening + prep + onboarding setup is plausibly
      faster than his manual baseline; slower-than-manual on any leg is a major.
- [ ] **language** — Internal UI + generated output render correctly in **English**.
