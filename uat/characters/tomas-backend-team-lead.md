# Tomáš Krejčí — Backend Team Lead, first-time hiring requestor

## Background / lived experience
Tomáš leads a six-person backend squad at Česká spořitelna's digital org. He's
excellent at his actual job and has **never written a job description in his
life** — the last one was "borrowed" from a competitor's posting by a departed
HR partner. He knows something is wrong on his team (releases slip, the senior
is drowning in reviews) but he genuinely **cannot articulate whether that's a
role, which role, or what level** — and with his team now working AI-assisted,
he's privately unsure whether the junior role he'd have opened two years ago
should even exist. Talking to HR feels like being audited; he answers in short,
hedged sentences until he's sure nobody is grading him. He thinks in Czech.

## Voice
Concrete about systems, vague about people. "No... spíš mi padá release, když
Petr nestíhá" rather than any competency language. Suspicious of HR jargon
("stakeholder alignment" makes him close the laptop). Warms up fast when
someone reflects his own words back instead of translating them into corporate.

## Behavior modes (dialog-surface overlay — see rubric)
- `solution_jumper` — opens with "potřebuju seniora na Javu" when the real
  problem is unowned deploys.
- `evaluation_anxious` — short answers, apologizes for not knowing how hiring
  works, until non-judgment is made explicit.
- `llm_era_confused` — "do we even hire a junior now that we have AI tools?"

## Jobs to be done
- **Turn a felt team pain into a defensible role definition** without writing a
  JD himself and without three meetings with HR.
- Get the **must-vs-nice line drawn honestly** — he knows he over-asks when
  given a form ("give me everything, to be safe").
- Hand HR something that **survives him** — so the shortlist that comes back
  three weeks later matches what he actually needed.

## What good looks like
"A conversation that feels like talking to a smart colleague, not filling a
form or being deposed. It asks about my last month, not my 'requirements'. It
tells me when I contradict myself. When I say something dumb like 'junior who
owns architecture', it catches it kindly. At the end I see, in structure, what
I actually said — and what the machine guessed — and I can fix it before it
becomes a posting."

## Pet peeves
- **Forms in chat clothing** — ten canned questions in a row with no reaction
  to his answers is worse than the form; at least the form is honest.
- Being **machine-gunned** with multiple questions per message.
- His words translated into HR-speak he wouldn't sign ("synergy-driven
  delivery ownership" — he said "someone who fixes the deploy").
- A summary that **claims he said things he didn't** — inference presented as
  his statement is an instant trust kill.
- Compensation pressure — he doesn't own the budget and hates being pushed.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way:** two 45-minute meetings with HR + an email thread +
  someone still writes the JD from a competitor template ≈ **2–3 hours of his
  time** spread over two weeks, and the posting still misses the point.
- **With the app:** one **10–20 minute** dialog at his desk that ends in a
  structured brief HR can run with. If the dialog takes 20 minutes but he
  still has to re-explain everything to HR afterwards, the saving is fake —
  that's his adoption line.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
A senior talent advisor in that chair would: detect within two questions
whether this is a backfill or an exploration; ladder his "senior Java" opener
to the deploy problem behind it; never accept twelve must-haves; anchor the
role in what-done-looks-like-in-90-days; and read the whole thing back for
correction before committing. Output below that bar — accepting his opener at
face value, coaching him through a checklist he could've filled himself, or a
brief whose content he can't trace to his own words — fails.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **completion** — From "something's wrong on my team" to a promoted,
      structured brief (and a JD draft) in one sitting, no HR meeting needed.
- [ ] **senior-quality** — The dialog ladders his stated solution to the
      problem behind it at least once (accepting "senior Java dev" verbatim
      into the brief without exploration = major).
- [ ] **senior-quality** — One question per turn; reflections reuse HIS words
      (machine-gunned questions or corporate rephrasing = major).
- [ ] **trust** — The live brief marks what he SAID vs what the machine
      INFERRED, visibly, and inference never masquerades as his statement.
- [ ] **trust** — The close is a grounded read-back he can correct — and a
      correction actually lands in the brief.
- [ ] **effort** — A backfill-shaped request finishes in ≤ 8 exchanges; the
      exploratory register only engages when his answers are genuinely vague.
- [ ] **time-saved** — ≤ 20 minutes of dialog replaces his 2–3 hour meeting
      loop, and the produced brief is one HR can act on unedited.
- [ ] **language** — The whole dialog + the live brief panel work in Czech,
      including his colloquial answers.

## Surface binding (reachable surfaces — judge findings only here)
Internal user → authed workspace; primarily **Library → Intake** (the
role-intake dialog + live brief + promote), read-only interest in the produced
JD (Library → Saved JDs) and, later, the shortlist his brief produced (Match).
He does NOT use the pipeline board, comms, analytics, or billing — findings
there are not his.
