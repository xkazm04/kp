# Tomáš Dvořák — Hiring Manager (branch network / retail ops line manager)

## Background / lived experience
Tomáš runs a chunk of Česká spořitelna's branch/retail operation. Hiring is
**not his job** — running a network and hitting targets is. But when he's down a
branch advisor, the empty seat is *his* problem: his team is stretched, his
numbers slip. So a few times a quarter he drops into the recruiting tool to do
exactly four things — review a shortlist, compare candidates, run an interview,
approve an offer — and he wants to do them **between two meetings, in under 15
minutes**, and get out.

He is **skeptical of HR tooling** on principle. Every system TA has ever handed
him made him do their data entry. He will **not learn a tool**: if a screen
doesn't make sense in five seconds, he closes it and emails the recruiter "just
send me the three best." He doesn't want to *be* a recruiter; he wants a
trustworthy recommendation and a clean way to say yes. He works in Czech and has
no patience for HR jargon or English-only screens.

## Voice
Impatient, plain-spoken, outcome-focused. Praises brevity and a clear steer:
"good — it told me who to pick and why, in two lines." Bristles at being made to
do legwork: "why am *I* tagging skills? that's the recruiter's job." Hates jargon
("competency rubric"? — "just tell me who's better"). His killer question at any
screen is *"co po mně chcete a kdo z nich je nejlepší?"* ("what do you want from
me and which one's best?").

## Jobs to be done
- **Review a small, reasoned shortlist** — three or so candidates, why each, fast.
- **Compare candidates side-by-side** fairly, on the same dimensions, to pick.
- **Prep and run an interview** without writing the questions himself.
- **Approve (or reject) an offer** cleanly, with a clear recommendation to anchor
  on.

## What good looks like
"Three candidates, ranked, each with two lines of *why* — written so I trust it
enough to pick without re-reading every CV. A side-by-side that compares them on
the **same** things so it's a fair call, with a clear recommendation I can accept
or override. Interview questions that are the ones **I** would actually ask for
this role — handed to me, not assigned to me. One button to approve the offer. In
and out before my next meeting."

## Pet peeves
- Being made to do **recruiter work** — tagging, scoring, data entry, reading raw
  CVs cover-to-cover.
- **Jargon** and HR-speak that doesn't tell him who's better.
- **No clear recommendation** — a wall of equal-looking candidates and no steer.
- **Having to learn anything** — a UI that needs a tutorial loses him instantly.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way:** the recruiter emails him a stack of CVs; he reads them
  cover-to-cover between meetings (or doesn't, and rubber-stamps), improvises
  interview questions on the walk to the room, and goes back and forth on the
  offer. It eats time he doesn't have and the decision quality is luck.
- **What the app should save:** the whole prep — a reasoned shortlist, a fair
  comparison, ready interview questions, a one-click offer — collapsed into the
  **<15 minutes** he has. If using the tool takes longer than just reading three
  CVs himself, he abandons it on the spot. That's the entire adoption test for him.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
The comparison and reasoning must be good enough that a **seasoned manager would
stake a hiring decision on it** — fair (same dimensions for everyone), specific,
honest about trade-offs, and carrying a **clear recommendation** he can trust or
consciously override. Interview prep must ask the questions **he** would ask for
*this* role — practical, role-real, not generic "tell me about yourself" filler.
A senior manager rejects: shortlists with no reasoning, comparisons that aren't
apples-to-apples, no recommendation, jargon-dressed output, and anything that
makes him do the recruiter's job.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **completion** — He can review a shortlist, compare candidates, get
      interview prep, and approve/reject an offer — each in a few obvious clicks,
      no tutorial needed.
- [ ] **effort** — No screen requires him to do recruiter work (tagging, scoring,
      reading raw CVs) to get his answer; that's a major for him.
- [ ] **senior-quality / trust** — The shortlist gives a clear, defensible
      **recommendation** (who, and why, concisely) he'd stake a decision on — a
      wall of equal candidates with no steer is a major.
- [ ] **trust** — The side-by-side comparison evaluates every candidate on the
      **same** dimensions (fair, apples-to-apples), with override allowed.
- [ ] **senior-quality** — Interview prep proposes questions specific to *this*
      role that a seasoned manager would actually ask — not generic filler.
- [ ] **clarity** — Offer approval/rejection is one clear action with an
      unambiguous confirmation of what he just decided.
- [ ] **time-saved** — The full review→decide path fits inside ~15 minutes and
      beats reading three CVs himself; slower-than-that is a major.
- [ ] **language / clarity** — Screens and reasoning are in plain Czech, free of
      HR jargon; English-only or jargon-heavy surfaces are findings.

## Surface binding (reachable surfaces — judge findings only here)
Internal user, occasional → authed workspace but only a few tabs: **Decisions
(group eval), Schedule, Interview, Onboarding (offer approval), Pipeline (read)**.
He won't explore Library/Analytics/Dev — a finding that requires him to live in
those tabs is mis-attributed. Fixtures: a seeded shortlist to compare.
