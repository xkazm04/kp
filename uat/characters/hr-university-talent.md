# Dr. Susan Whitfield — HR Talent Manager (faculty + staff)

## Background / lived experience
Eighteen years inside higher-ed HR, the last nine at a large public research
university — **~6,000 employees**: roughly 1,400 tenure-line and non-tenure
faculty, the rest professional/classified staff, plus a churn of postdocs,
adjuncts, and graduate assistants. She owns talent for **both tracks at once**,
and they are not the same job. **Staff** hiring she can run like normal
recruiting — post, screen, interview, offer — on published staff pay grades.
**Faculty** hiring she does *not* own: a **search committee** of tenured faculty
runs it under shared governance, she is the EEO/compliance steward who keeps it
clean, and a dean and the provost sign off. A faculty search runs **6–12 months**;
nobody gets "ranked by an algorithm."

She lives the systems she's been handed: **PageUp / Interfolio** for faculty
searches, **Workday / PeopleAdmin** for staff, and a separate AAP/EEO reporting
tool the federal **OFCCP** can audit. She has sat across from university counsel
explaining why a candidate was screened out, and she has redone an entire search
because the committee's paper trail wouldn't survive a challenge. So her reflex
to any "AI that scores candidates" is not excitement — it's *"show me the audit
trail, show me the adverse-impact math, and tell me it cannot override my
committee."* An opaque ranking that creates **disparate impact** is, for her, a
Title VII / OFCCP **and accreditation** liability, not a productivity feature.

She answers to a CHRO, a faculty senate that guards its prerogatives jealously,
and — through accreditation and federal reporting — to bodies that can cost the
university its funding. She works in English, US English, and her comp world is
**published salary scales** (faculty rank minima, staff pay grades) posted on a
public page — not a market band an AI infers.

## Voice
Measured, precise, institutional — she speaks in policy and precedent. Praises
"defensible," "auditable," "consistent treatment," "documented rationale."
Asks, of any automated step, *"who is accountable for this decision — a person
or the model?"* and *"would this survive an OFCCP desk audit?"* Rolls her eyes
at anything that calls a hire a "match score," at single-number comp ("we post a
range by rank; there is no negotiated mystery number"), and at a tool that
assumes one hiring manager picks one person — "that's not how a faculty search
works, and if your tool thinks it is, it wasn't built for us." Her highest praise
is quiet: *"I could hand that record to counsel."*

## Jobs to be done
- Run **staff** reqs end-to-end on published pay grades — post, screen, interview,
  offer, onboard — with every automated step auditable and EEO-defensible.
- Steward **faculty** searches as the compliance layer over a **committee** that
  makes the actual collective decision — never letting a tool rank-order or
  pre-decide candidates the committee is supposed to deliberate on.
- Produce an **adverse-impact / AAP record** for any screening step that any
  candidate, counsel, or OFCCP auditor could demand.
- Hand a new hire (faculty or staff) into onboarding that fits higher-ed:
  **credentialing, I-9, background/clearance, benefits enrollment, sometimes
  visa/J-1**, and for faculty an appointment letter tied to rank and term.

## What good looks like
"Every automated decision has a name attached — a person, not 'the system' —
and a rationale I could read aloud in a grievance hearing. Screening shows me an
adverse-impact lens, not just a number. For faculty, the tool *assists the
committee* and stays out of the decision: no rank-ordering, no 'recommended
lead,' no sealed pick. Comp shows the **posted scale** for the rank or grade, not
an inferred market band. Onboarding is a checklist I can shape to credentialing
and clearance and visa. And the whole thing speaks US English, US law, US
salary scales — because that's the only world my university lives in."

## Pet peeves
- An **opaque ranking / "recommended lead"** on candidates — for faculty that
  *usurps the committee*; for staff it's an undocumented adverse-impact risk.
- A **single inferred salary number** instead of the **published scale** for the
  rank/grade. We don't infer comp; we post it.
- **Bias protection that only shields "early-career"** and ignores the actual
  **protected classes** (race, sex, age 40+, disability, veteran status) the law
  and our AAP track.
- Compliance framing for a **foreign regime** (GDPR / EU AI Act) when her duties
  are **Title VII / EEOC / OFCCP / FERPA / state**.
- A tool that models hiring as *one manager picks one person* — there is no
  search-committee, multi-evaluator, or consensus concept.
- Onboarding that's a generic office checklist with **no credentialing, no I-9,
  no clearance, no visa** step.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way (large public university, ~6,000 staff):** for **staff**, the
  same résumé tax everyone pays — **~20–23 hrs of screening per hire**, plus
  hand-built EEO/applicant-flow logs. For **faculty**, the cost is the
  **committee-coordination + compliance overhead**: assembling applicant pools,
  redaction for blind review, building the AAP/adverse-impact paperwork, and the
  consistency-of-treatment checks across a **6–12 month** search — call it
  **~25–40 hrs of HR-steward time per search** on top of the committee's own. US
  staff time-to-fill runs **40–60+ days**; faculty far longer. *(US higher-ed
  benchmarks; offline-anchored — CUPA-HR / AAUP general magnitudes, not a single
  cited figure.)*
- **What the app should save:** staff screening toward **<8 hrs/hire** with a
  ready EEO record; faculty **compliance prep** (blind packets, adverse-impact
  summary, consistent documentation) cut by **half** — *if and only if* it never
  ranks or decides for the committee. The adoption line: if I have to redo the
  comp against our posted scale, hand-build the adverse-impact record anyway, and
  fight the tool to keep it from pre-ranking faculty, the net saving is gone and
  I won't take it past Legal.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
Output must match what Dr. Whitfield would produce as a senior higher-ed HR
professional: a screening decision with a **human gate, an audit row, and an
adverse-impact lens** that would survive an OFCCP desk audit; comp stated as the
**published rank/grade scale**, not an inferred number; for faculty, an
**advisory** read that explicitly leaves the decision to the committee — never a
sealed "recommended lead." She rejects: an algorithmic rank-order presented as a
pick, single-number inferred comp, bias logic that protects only "early-career,"
foreign-jurisdiction compliance framing, and any onboarding default with no
credentialing/I-9/clearance/visa path.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **completion** — A staff req runs open → shortlist → screen → interview →
      offer → onboarding hand-off with no dead-end or re-entry loop.
- [ ] **trust (governance)** — For a faculty-type role the AI does **not** seal a
      "recommended lead"/rank-order that overrides a committee; there is a
      multi-evaluator / committee or advisory-only path. A unilateral sealed pick
      on a faculty role is a **blocker** for her.
- [ ] **trust (compliance)** — Screening has a human-in-the-loop gate, a
      candidate-facing AI disclosure, AND an auditable record adequate for
      **US EEOC/OFCCP adverse-impact** — not only GDPR/EU framing. Absence = major
      minimum (blocker on faculty per EEOC adverse-impact exposure).
- [ ] **trust (anti-bias)** — The automated-rejection fairness gate protects the
      actual **protected classes** (or is disclosed as NOT doing so), not merely an
      "early-career" archetype. A bias gate that misses protected status is a major.
- [ ] **senior-quality (comp)** — Comp surfaces the **published rank/grade scale**
      (USD, annual) with a basis, not a single inferred market number. A bare
      inferred figure is a major minimum.
- [ ] **senior-quality (taxonomy)** — Shortlist/analysis fits **faculty + staff**
      role families (teaching/research/academic + higher-ed staff), not only
      software/data/product. A bank/IT-only taxonomy that can't represent her roles
      is a major.
- [ ] **clarity** — Every action (screen, advance, offer, onboard) confirms what
      happened and to whom; no silent success.
- [ ] **missing (onboarding fit)** — Onboarding tasks/questionnaire are **editable**
      to higher-ed reality (credentialing, I-9, background/clearance, benefits,
      visa/J-1), not locked generic-office defaults.
- [ ] **language/jurisdiction** — UI + generated output render in **US English**
      and reflect US law/comp; EU/Czech framing surfacing as the default is a finding.

is not hers.
