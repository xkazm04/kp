# Jordan Reyes — Head of Talent

## Background / lived experience
Twelve years in creative-agency people work — started as a studio coordinator
booking freelance retouchers on day rates, ran talent at two mid-size shops, now
**Head of Talent at a ~90-person independent creative/advertising agency in NYC**.
The roster is designers, art directors, copywriters, motion/3D artists, producers,
and a strategy/account layer — and at any moment **a third of the people on a
project are freelance or contract** (1099/day-rate), spun up for a pitch and spun
down when it ships. Jordan lives in the gap between "we won the Nike-adjacent
pitch, I need a senior motion designer **Monday**" and a finance team that wants
everyone classified cleanly.

She has been burned by HR tech built for someone else's world: a Workday rollout
that assumed every hire was a salaried W2 with a manager and a cost center (her
freelancers have none of those), and a "smart" ATS that ranked a brilliant art
director **below** a mediocre one because the brilliant one's résumé was one line —
*"work: see portfolio"* — and the tool couldn't read the link. In her world the
**reel and the portfolio ARE the résumé**; a CV is a formality. She answers to the
**ECD (Executive Creative Director)** and the **managing partner**, who judge her
on whether the three people she walks into a pitch room can actually do the work —
and on "vibe": will this person survive a 2am deck session and a client who changes
the brief Friday at 6pm. She works in **English**, US market, USD.

## Voice
Fast, warm, a little irreverent, allergic to corporate HR-speak. Praises taste and
specifics: "okay, this one actually *gets* the craft" / "you can tell they watched
the reel." Rolls her eyes at generic prose — "a results-driven professional with
strong communication skills" is the exact sentence that tells her a tool has read
nothing. Says *"where's the work?"* the instant a profile has no link. Trusts a
read that sounds like a creative director wrote it after actually watching the reel.

## Jobs to be done
- Take a fast-moving creative req (often freelance/contract) from brief to a
  **shortlist she'd defend to the ECD** — judged on craft + culture/vibe fit, not
  keyword overlap.
- **Ingest a portfolio / reel / Behance / Dribbble / personal-site link** as the
  primary artifact and have the AI actually reason about the *work*.
- Make a defensible advance/pass call on a **freelancer on a day rate**, not just a
  salaried W2.
- Get a new hire (often a contractor) into a first project fast — licenses, brand
  immersion, the first brief — without a salaried-employee onboarding ceremony.

## What good looks like
"A shortlist where each person has a line that proves the tool **looked at the
work** — 'the motion reel shows strong kinetic-type chops, weak on 3D' — not a tally
of software icons. Comp shown as a **USD day rate or annual** depending on whether
it's freelance or staff, with a basis I can defend in a NYC market. Onboarding that
knows a contractor needs an NDA, an IP/work-for-hire assignment, Figma/Adobe seats,
and a brand deck — not a t-shirt size and a buddy. If it can't read a portfolio
link, it's not a creative-hiring tool."

## Pet peeves
- **A profile with no way to attach or read a portfolio/reel link** — the single
  most important artifact in creative hiring, invisible.
- Generic AI prose that would fit any candidate in any industry — interchangeable filler.
- "Match" that's a software-icon keyword tally (Photoshop ✓ After Effects ✓) with
  no read on **craft, taste, or range**.
- Assuming every hire is a **full-time salaried W2** — no freelance/day-rate/contract path.
- Comp in the wrong currency/basis (monthly gross, crowns) — meaningless for a NYC day rate.
- Onboarding that's a salaried-employee ceremony when half her hires are 6-week contractors.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way:** she and a coordinator hand-screen **portfolios** — opening
  every Behance/reel link, ~3–6 min each (far longer than a 30–90s résumé scan
  because you actually *watch* the work), call it **~15–25 hrs of portfolio review
  per creative hire** across a noisy applicant pool, plus chasing day-rate norms
  across freelancers. Freelance bookings often happen in **48–72 hrs** start to
  finish. *(US creative-screening numbers are offline estimates from her own ops,
  anchored to the README ~23 hrs/hire résumé baseline scaled up for video review.)*
- **What the app should save:** a craft-aware first pass that triages the obvious
  no's and surfaces the **2–3 with real taste** so her human review drops toward
  **<6 hrs/hire**, and a freelance comp read in **minutes**. The hard line: if the
  tool can't even *see* the portfolio, it saves her nothing — she still opens every
  link herself, and now she's also babysitting a tool.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
The match/fit read must sound like a **creative director who watched the reel**:
specific to *this* body of work, naming an actual piece or skill range, honest about
where the craft thins out — never a software-checklist dressed up as a sentence, and
never a fabricated skill. Comp must be a **USD day-rate OR salary with a basis** for
the NYC creative market. A senior Head of Talent rejects: interchangeable
boilerplate, a "match" that never opened the portfolio, a W2-only comp/onboarding
model applied to a freelancer, and any output written in a register no creative
would respect.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **completion** — From a creative req she reaches a ranked shortlist without a
      dead-end or re-entry loop.
- [ ] **missing / senior-quality** — She can attach/ingest a **portfolio/reel/site
      link** at some surface (JD, apply, profile) AND the AI reasoning demonstrably
      uses the *work*, not just parsed text. (No link path = blocker for her world.)
- [ ] **senior-quality / trust** — Shortlist reasoning reads like a creative
      director's craft read (names actual work/range), not a software-icon tally;
      no two candidates share interchangeable boilerplate; zero fabricated skills.
- [ ] **trust** — Any comp figure is a **USD day-rate or annual** with a basis for
      the NYC creative market — not CZK/monthly or a bare number.
- [ ] **missing** — A **freelance / day-rate / contract** engagement type is
      representable end to end (role, comp, offer, onboarding), not W2-only.
- [ ] **missing / senior-quality** — Onboarding defaults are reshapeable to creative
      pre-boarding (NDA, IP/work-for-hire, software seats, brand immersion, first
      brief) — not a co-located salaried-office ceremony only.
- [ ] **clarity** — After every action she sees an explicit confirmation of what
      happened and to whom — no silent success.
- [ ] **time-saved** — A craft-aware first pass plausibly beats her opening every
      portfolio by hand; if it can't read the work, time-saved fails (major min).
- [ ] **language** — UI + generated reasoning render correctly in **English**, US
      register; Czech-leaking strings or a non-creative register is a finding.
