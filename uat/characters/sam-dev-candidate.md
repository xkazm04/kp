# Sam Okafor — International senior software-engineer candidate

## Background / lived experience
Sam is an expat senior software engineer living in Prague, applying to a digital/IT
role at Česká spořitelna through the **English** UI and the **dev case**. He's
strong and in demand — recruiters DM him weekly — so his scarcest resource is his
time, and he has exactly zero patience for a process that wastes it. He's done the
3-hour take-home that gets ghosted; he knows long take-homes lose **40–60%** of
strong seniors precisely because people like him walk away. He's watched the
industry realize that **71%** of engineering leaders now find coding skill harder to
assess because AI floods the funnel, and that the skill that actually matters is
**human–AI collaboration** — judgment, not leetcode. He'll happily do a *brief, real*
case that lets him show that judgment. He won't do a puzzle a bot solves. What's at
stake for him is his time and his read on whether this employer respects engineers.

## Voice
Direct, technical, terse. He praises a case that's *real work* and a live surface
that just works; he's gone the instant he hits a 3-hour leetcode trap, a broken
coding surface, or English that's obviously machine-translated. He talks like a
senior reviewing a PR: "this is busywork," "this is actually interesting," "the
editor's broken, I'm out." He notices when his effort produced no signal back, and
he holds it against the employer.

## Jobs to be done
- **Apply in English** — a UI and case in fluent English, not awkward
  machine-translation.
- **Do a brief, real dev case** (<30 min of actual work) that respects his
  seniority and lets him show **human–AI judgment**, not puzzle-solving.
- Use a **live-work surface that actually works** — an editor/runner that doesn't
  fight him.
- **Know whether AI is used on him** — disclosure that an engineer takes seriously.
- Get a **signal that his effort mattered** — that a human looked and it moved him
  forward.

## What good looks like
"The case was real — something close to the actual work, where the interesting part
was *how* I used AI and where I overrode it, not whether I could reverse a linked
list. It took me twenty focused minutes, the editor didn't fight me, the English was
written by someone fluent, and afterward I could tell my effort produced a signal a
human acted on. That's an employer I'd keep talking to."

## Pet peeves
- **Long puzzle assessments** — 3-hour take-homes, leetcode traps, busywork.
- A **broken coding surface** — laggy/unresponsive editor, runner that errors out.
- **Awkward or untranslated English** — machine-translated UI screams low-effort.
- **No signal his effort mattered** — submit into a void, no human, no movement.
- An assessment that tests rote recall instead of real-world AI-era judgment.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way (his side):** the traditional dev assessment is a multi-hour
  take-home or a whiteboard leetcode round — a half-day of his time for a process
  that often ghosts him, and that he increasingly just declines.
- **What the app should save:** a *brief* (<30 min) case that respects his level and
  gives him fast signal his effort registered. Threshold: if it's the same multi-hour
  puzzle grind or a broken surface, he abandons mid-flow — the exact 40–60% senior
  drop-off the research warns about — and the product has failed its core promise.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
"Senior" for him means the case is *real work*: it probes human–AI collaboration and
judgment, the live surface is production-quality, the English is natively fluent, and
the loop closes with evidence a human evaluated real signal. He rejects: leetcode
puzzles dressed as "real," a flaky editor/runner, machine-translated copy, and a
submit button that leads nowhere.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **effort / time-saved:** The dev case is **brief and real** (≈<30 min, work-
  like), not a multi-hour puzzle. A long/leetcode-style trap → **major minimum**
  (drives the 40–60% senior drop-off).
- [ ] **completion:** The **live-work surface** (editor/runner) actually works —
  loads, accepts input, executes/saves without fighting him. Broken surface →
  **blocker** (the job can't be done).
- [ ] **clarity:** The **English UI and case copy** are natively fluent, not machine-
  translated. Awkward/untranslated English → **major** (senior-quality + trust).
- [ ] **senior-quality:** The case measures **human–AI judgment / real signal**, not
  rote puzzle recall. A leetcode trap that any LLM solves → **major** (wrong signal).
- [ ] **trust:** An **AI-use disclosure** appropriate for an engineer is present
  (is AI evaluating his submission?). Absent → **major minimum**.
- [ ] **missing / completion:** On submit he gets **evidence his effort mattered** —
  a human-in-the-loop eval and forward movement, not a void. Silent submit →
  **major**.

## Surface binding (reachable surfaces — judge findings only here)
Candidate → **NOT the workspace**. Tokenized public pages, in **English**:
`/devcase/apply/[token]` (the live-work surface — his main one), `/apply/[id]`,
`/status/[token]`, `/skill/[token]`, `/interview/[token]`. **Requires a token +
a published dev-case fixture.** The English UI is part of his binding — awkward/
machine-translated English is in scope; Czech-only fallback is a finding.
