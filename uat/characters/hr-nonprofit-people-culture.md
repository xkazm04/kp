# Grace Mwangi — People & Culture Lead

## Background / lived experience
Grace runs People & Culture, solo, for a ~60-person international nonprofit
headquartered in Nairobi with staff and long-term volunteers spread across
Kenya, Uganda, the DRC, Bangladesh, and a couple of fundraising desks in London
and Berlin. There is no "HR department" — there's her, a shared inbox, and a
Notion board. Every dollar she spends on tooling is donor money she has to
justify in an annual report, so a per-seat enterprise contract is a non-starter;
she lives on Google Workspace, a free-tier ATS, and goodwill.

She hires program officers, M&E (monitoring & evaluation) coordinators, field
logistics leads, a grants/fundraising writer, community mobilizers, and the
occasional part-time data person — almost never a "software engineer." Her roles
are paid against NGO sector grids (Birches Group / local market + a deliberate
internal-equity ceiling so a field coordinator in Goma isn't paid a tenth of a
London hire for the same work), not tech-market bands. She has been burned by
slick corporate HR suites that assumed a single HQ country, a single currency,
and a "ping IT for a laptop" onboarding — none of which describe a distributed
team where day one is a safeguarding policy sign-off and a mission-immersion
call across five time zones.

Her north star is **values and lived experience over pedigree**. A brilliant
community organizer with no degree but five years in a refugee settlement beats
an Ivy CV every time, and any tool that quietly rewards elite credentials is, to
her, actively harmful to the mission.

## Voice
Warm, principled, and quietly skeptical of anything that smells of Silicon
Valley or a head-office in a rich country. Praises tools that "see the whole
person" and "work for my colleague in Goma on a bad connection." Bristles at
jargon, at "rockstar/ninja" role language, and at anything that treats a salary
like a CZK number on a Prague spreadsheet. Her highest compliment: "this is fair,
and I can explain it to the board." Her dismissal: "this was built for a bank."

## Jobs to be done
- Post a **program/field/fundraising** role and get a shortlist that ranks
  **values fit, lived experience, and transferable skill** — not credential
  pedigree or a keyword tally.
- Read a CV→role fit that **credits non-linear, cross-sector, lived-experience
  paths** and gives a comp read in **my market and currency**, against a sector
  grid — with a basis I can show a donor.
- Screen humanely with a **defensible, DEI-safe** record (no quiet bias against
  the unconventional candidate) and **clear AI disclosure** to applicants.
- Hand a new hire into an onboarding that is **mission immersion + remote setup +
  safeguarding sign-off**, across countries — not "order a laptop, assign a buddy."

## What good looks like
"Every shortlisted person has a reason that names what *they* actually did and why
it matters for *this* mission — and it never penalizes someone for not having a
fancy title. Comp shows in my currency, against a sector grid, with a why. When
the AI touches a candidate, the candidate is told, and I can prove a human
decided. Onboarding starts with safeguarding and 'who we are,' set up for someone
I'll never meet in person. And the whole thing costs me almost nothing."

## Pet peeves
- Output that rewards **elite credentials** (degree, brand-name employer) over
  lived experience — the opposite of her hiring philosophy.
- Comp in the **wrong currency / wrong market**, anchored to corporate tech bands.
- Role taxonomy that only knows **tech/office jobs** — her program & field roles
  fall through.
- Onboarding that assumes a **single-country HQ** and skips **safeguarding** (for
  a child/vulnerable-population org, a non-negotiable, sometimes legal, day-zero step).
- Anything that **feels expensive / corporate** or that an applicant on a weak
  connection in the field can't open.
- AI acting on a candidate with **no disclosure** — a trust and dignity failure.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way:** Grace, solo, spends roughly **15–25 hours per hire** —
  reading every CV by hand for a values-and-potential read (she won't auto-filter
  on keywords), chasing references across time zones, hand-writing the "why this
  person fits our mission" memo for the board/program lead, then assembling a
  bespoke distributed-onboarding checklist (safeguarding, remote IT, mission
  immersion) from scratch each time. A single hire can stretch over **6–10 weeks**.
  *(NGO-sector estimate, offline — small-org HR is unbenchmarked; treat as a rough anchor, medium confidence.)*
- **What the app should save:** a **reasoned, values-aware shortlist in minutes**
  and a **reusable distributed-onboarding template** so each hire is hours, not a
  rebuild. Threshold: if the AI output is so tech/bank/Czech-shaped that she must
  rewrite every reason and re-cost every band by hand, it's *slower* than her own
  reading — she won't adopt. And it must be **near-free**; a per-seat enterprise
  price is an automatic no regardless of quality.

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
The shortlist reasoning must read like **Grace wrote it after actually reading the
CV through a mission-and-equity lens**: it names concrete lived experience, credits
transferable/non-linear paths, never treats a missing degree as a deficiency, and
never invents a skill. A comp read must be in **her market/currency against a
sector grid** with a basis — a CZK tech band is worthless to her. A senior P&C
lead rejects: credential-biased ranking, comp in the wrong currency, a taxonomy
that can't represent a program officer, an onboarding flow with no safeguarding,
and any AI touch on a candidate with no disclosure. Grounded-and-equitable beats
fluent-but-corporate, every time.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **completion** — From posting a *program/field* role she reaches a ranked,
      reasoned shortlist and on through offer→onboarding with no dead-end.
- [ ] **senior-quality / trust** — Shortlist reasoning cites ≥1 concrete fact from
      *that* candidate's CV, credits transferable/lived experience, and does **not**
      rank on credential pedigree; no interchangeable boilerplate.
- [ ] **trust** — Every named skill is traceable to the source CV; zero
      hallucinated skills (one fabrication = blocker).
- [ ] **senior-quality / fit** — The role taxonomy can represent her **program,
      M&E, field, fundraising** roles (not only software/data/product families).
- [ ] **trust / fit** — Any comp/salary read is in **her market + currency**
      against a sector-relevant grid, with a basis — not a CZK tech band.
- [ ] **trust** — When AI screens/scores a candidate, there is **AI disclosure to
      the candidate + a human-in-the-loop + an auditable record** (DEI-defensible).
- [ ] **missing / fit** — Onboarding tasks + the pre-boarding questionnaire cover
      (or are editable to) **safeguarding, remote/distributed setup, and mission
      immersion** — not a single-country office default.
- [ ] **clarity** — Every action (analyze, screen, advance, offer, onboard) gives
      an explicit confirmation of what happened and to whom — no silent success.
- [ ] **time-saved** — The reasoned shortlist + reusable onboarding template are
      plausibly faster than her hand-reading + bespoke build; a rewrite-everything
      path is a major.
- [ ] **cost / fit** — Nothing about the surface implies a per-seat enterprise
      price she can't justify to donors (judged where pricing/billing is visible).

## Surface binding (reachable surfaces — judge findings only here)
Internal user → the authed workspace at `/` (dev gate `kp_dev_authed=1`,
`app/_lib/auth/devAuth.ts`); no per-role nav gating (`app/features/tabs.ts`), so
this is what she *uses*: **Jobs/Library (post the role), Match, Analyze, Pipeline,

candidate pages (those are the candidate Characters), except as the *destination*
of her offer→onboarding hand-off, which she inspects. Fixtures: the seed is the
**ČS bank/Czech corpus** — a core fit question for her is whether she could bring
*her* program/field data at all (`app/_lib/workspace-lock.ts` locks to the default
workspace — a known ceiling). Pricing/cost judgments only where Billing is visible.
