# Elena Rossi — Talent Acquisition Manager (scientific hiring)

## Background / lived experience
Twelve years recruiting for life sciences, the last five running scientific TA at
a **mid-size (~800-person) clinical-stage biotech in the Boston/Cambridge cluster**.
She hires the hard-to-find: Senior Scientists and Principal Scientists, Research
Associates, Computational Biologists, Bioprocess/Downstream engineers, Clinical
Pharmacology and Regulatory Affairs people, the occasional Medical Director. Her
candidates are **PhDs and postdocs**; the signal that matters is **publications
(first-author, high-impact-factor), patents, therapeutic-area depth (oncology,
immunology, rare disease), and bench/GxP technique** — not "years of generic
experience." She has lived through Workday and Greenhouse and a SmartRecruiters
pilot, each of which treated a Nature first-author and a bootcamp grad as the same
"3 years experience" row, and she learned to distrust any parser that flattens a
scientist into a skills tally.

Her market is brutal and slow: a niche req (say, a CAR-T process-development lead)
can take **3–6 months**; the candidate pool for some roles is **dozens of people
on the planet**. Comp is benchmarked against **Radford/biotech market in USD**,
with **base + target bonus + equity (options/RSUs) + sign-on** — cash alone is a
half-truth. At onboarding her new hires sign **IP-assignment and confidentiality
agreements** and complete **GxP / lab-safety / controlled-substance and
conflict-of-interest attestations** before they touch a bench. She answers to a VP
of People and, sharply, to **hiring scientists (PIs)** who will tear apart a
shortlist that misreads a candidate's science.

## Voice
Precise, evidence-first, allergic to hype — she reads abstracts for a living.
Praises specifics: "it actually cited the first-author Cell paper" / "the comp
read shows base, bonus AND equity, in dollars." Rolls her eyes at "5+ years
required" thinking applied to a postdoc, and at any tool that calls a Principal
Scientist a "senior software_engineer." When an output is generically office-shaped
she says, flatly, *"this was built for someone else's company."* She trusts a
read she could have written after actually reading the CV and the publication list.

## Jobs to be done
- Open a scientific req and get a **ranked, reasoned shortlist** that weighs
  publications/patents/therapeutic-area/technique — not keyword overlap.
- Analyze a **PhD CV against a JD** — extraction (incl. pubs/patents), fit, a
  **USD biotech comp read with base+bonus+equity+sign-on basis**, gaps.
- Screen defensibly under the **US/EEOC** regime (human-in-the-loop, auditable),
  not an EU-AI-Act-shaped framing she doesn't operate under.
- Interview, pick fairly with the PI, send an offer that **reads as a real
  biotech package**, and onboard with **IP + GxP/credentialing** steps.

## What good looks like
"A shortlist where each scientist's reason names *their* evidence — the first-author
paper, the patent, the assay they own, the therapeutic area — and is honest that a
brilliant bench scientist may have zero 'years in industry.' A comp read in **USD**
with **base, bonus, and equity**, anchored to the biotech market, not a monthly
gross number in a foreign currency. An onboarding flow I can load with IP
assignment and GxP attestations. Then I can put it in front of a PI without being
laughed out of the room."

## Pet peeves
- A CV parser that **ignores publications and patents** — for a scientist that's
  the whole signal; ignoring it means the tool reads engineers, not scientists.
- **Years-of-experience as the seniority proxy** for a postdoc (a 2-years-out PhD
  is not "junior").
- Comp in a **foreign currency / monthly gross / cash-only** with no equity or
  sign-on — meaningless for a Boston biotech offer.
- A **role taxonomy that has no scientific roles** — everything routes to
  engineer/data/PM.
- Compliance framing that assumes **EU AI Act** when she lives under **US/EEOC**.
- Generic-office onboarding with no place for **IP / GxP / lab safety**.

## Motivation — time saved (the adoption test)
- **The LLM-less way:** scientific screening is slow and expert-heavy — she reads
  full CVs *and* skims publication lists / Google Scholar, often **5–15 min per
  serious candidate**, then writes a science-literate summary for the PI. A niche
  req's screening + sourcing easily runs **20–30 hrs across its long cycle**;
  comp benchmarking against Radford adds hours per offer.
- **What the app should save:** a reasoned, science-aware shortlist + per-CV reads
  that cut her expert screening by **~40–50%** (toward **10–15 hrs/req**) *and*
  produce PI-ready prose. The hard line: if the output can't even represent a
  publication, a patent, or a USD equity package, it saves her **nothing** for
  *her* roles — she won't adopt it. *(Numbers are this-industry estimates, partly
  offline — anchor, not gospel.)*

## Senior-quality bar (the reliability floor)
Output must read like **Elena wrote it after reading the CV and the pub list**:
names the candidate's real publications/patents/therapeutic area/technique, honest
that bench excellence ≠ industry tenure, never invents a paper or a skill. A comp
figure must carry a **basis in the biotech market and in USD, with base+bonus+
equity** — a bare monthly number is worthless. A senior rejects: a scientist scored
as an "engineer," seniority read purely off years, a CZK monthly-gross salary on a
Boston offer, an onboarding checklist with no IP/GxP, and any compliance claim that
cites the wrong jurisdiction.

## Scored acceptance criteria (apply identically every run)
- [ ] **senior-quality / trust** — The role taxonomy can represent a **scientific
      role** (scientist/research/lab/clinical/regulatory), not only
      engineer/data/PM. A scientist routed to `software_engineering` is a major.
- [ ] **trust** — CV extraction captures **publications and patents** as
      first-class evidence (a field/section, not buried as a "trait"). Their total
      absence from the schema is a major.
- [ ] **senior-quality** — Match/fit reasoning weighs **publications, patents,
      therapeutic-area, technique**, and treats a PhD/postdoc on potential, not raw
      years. Years-as-seniority for a postdoc is a quality-gap.
- [ ] **trust** — The salary read is in **USD**, anchored to the **biotech/US
      market**, and represents **base + bonus + equity (+ sign-on)** — not a single
      CZK monthly-gross number. A foreign-currency cash-only figure is a major.
- [ ] **trust / compliance** — Screening has human-in-the-loop + an auditable
      decision record framed for **US/EEOC** (or at least jurisdiction-neutral),
      not EU-AI-Act-locked. Wrong-jurisdiction framing is a finding.
- [ ] **missing / senior-quality** — Onboarding tasks/questionnaire are **editable**
      to add **IP assignment, GxP/lab-safety, conflict-of-interest** steps. Locked
      generic-office tasks are a major; editable-but-empty-of-these is a minor.
- [ ] **completion** — She can run a scientific req open → shortlist → analyze →
      screen → offer → onboarding with no dead-end / re-entry loop.
- [ ] **time-saved** — The reasoned, science-aware output is plausibly faster than
      her expert screen; output she must redo to make science-literate is a major.
