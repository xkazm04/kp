> Moonshots: 5 (Tier1/2/3: 3/2/0)

# Assessment & Autonomous Operation — Moonshots

**Cluster question**: *can we make a remote work-sample assessment that is fraud-proof by design, and run an autonomous recruiter that is trusted because it is measured — not because we assert it.*

## What genuinely exists today (grounding read)

This cluster is already unusually principled, which is exactly why the moonshots must aim past "more probes" or "more autonomy":

- **The assessment thesis is already post-authorship.** `pipeline/jobfit/devcase/design.py` (`CASE_DESIGN_PROMPT_VERSION = "case-design-v4"`) plants `CoverProbe`s (`ambiguity`, `legacy_trap`, `verification_trap`) each carrying a `decisionSpace` (2–3 defensible options) and an internal `reveals`. The case forces a mandatory `DECISIONS.md` log. The stance: *never grade AI use; grade judgment.* Authorship is triangulated **post-hoc** in a live interview via `evaluate.py:mint_followups` (anchored questions with `listenFor`/`redFlag`).
- **The trace is metadata-only, the verdict is structural.** `pipeline/jobfit/devcase/reflect.py` (`CommitReflection`: `iterationPattern`, `readBeforeWrite`, `verificationHabits`; `assess_tooling` → `ToolingSignal` with `overRelianceFlags` that **may never be invented from tool use alone**). There is **no code execution, no runtime, no keystroke capture, no plagiarism/clone detection** — by deliberate design, the system assumes 100% LLM code is legitimate.
- **Fairness is gated against a synthetic landscape, not measured against reality.** `submission_eval.py` runs 6 hand-authored behavior personas (`careful_verifier` … `ai_no_verify` "gamer") through `fairness`/`discrimination` gates (`MIN_VERIFY_MARGIN`, `AI_PENALTY_TOLERANCE`). These personas are *authored constants in `submission_scenarios.py`*, not generative — and they never touch a real adversary.
- **Autonomy is a narrow, human-gated state machine.** `app/_lib/devcase-orchestrator.ts:runLifecycle` walks 10 stages; `DEV_POLICY` (`promoteFloor: 55`, `promoteTopN: 3`) + `gateApproval(analysis)` decide auto-approve vs. route-to-human; `app/_lib/dev-control.ts` is a binary `Autonomy = "on" | "paused"` kill-switch with an immutable `dev_audit` log. `app/_lib/dev-outcomes.ts:calibrate()` already buckets real hire outcomes into fixed score bands and *suggests* a promote-floor — but a human must apply it, and it tunes exactly one scalar.
- **The "autonomous recruiter" today is a deterministic rule engine.** `app/_lib/automation-pass.ts:runAutomationPass` + `pipeline/jobfit/automation.py:evaluate_entry` apply per-entry advance/reject/hold with a defense-in-depth fairness backstop (`automation-fairness.ts:assertAutoRejectFair`, `BAU_REJECT_SCORE = 40`). `scheduler.ts:tickScheduler` claims due runs. There is **no learning loop, no narration of intent, no per-candidate negotiation** — autonomy is "execute the fixed POLICY dict on a clock."
- **The demo simulation is a scripted single-candidate walk.** `SimulationProvider.tsx:run()` choreographs 7 phases (`design → … → hired`) over real UI/API with one `SIM_SCREEN_POLICY`-coupled applicant. It proves the happy path; it never stresses the engine with a *population* or an *adversary*.
- **Apply is a scripted stepper, not a dialog.** `app/apply/[id]/ConversationalApply.tsx` is a conditional stepper (CV-autofill, save-resume, archetype lanes) with **no LLM dialog, no live-match feedback, and no continuity into assessment** — the candidate hands off a repo URL with zero in-product work surface (`devcase/apply/[token]/SeedFiles.tsx` is download-only).

**The structural gap the moonshots attack**: the assessment engine is *defended by assumption and authored personas*, never proven against a real adversary; the autonomous engine is *asserted to be fair*, never measured outcome-by-outcome; and the candidate never does the actual work *inside the product*, so the highest-trust signal (live process) is left on the table. Every moonshot below converts an **assumption into a measured, observed, or adversarially-proven artifact.**

---

## 1. **The Live Work Surface — move the take-home INSIDE the product and capture the process, not the artifact**
- **Tier**: 1 (10x category-defining)
- **Category**: assessment-redefined
- **Impact**: For every candidate and every hiring team. Today the entire authenticity apparatus (`reflect.py`, the `DECISIONS.md` contract, the post-hoc interview) is a *reconstruction* from a git log the candidate produces in private — the one place AI-vs-human is genuinely ambiguous. The 10x change: the candidate works the case in an embedded, ephemeral workspace (browser editor + scoped agent + sandboxed run) reached straight from `devcase/apply/[token]`, and the engine observes the **real process** — what they read before writing, which probe seams they opened, how they reacted when a test they wrote went red, whether they pushed back on the agent. This collapses "infer the mental model from commit messages" into "watch the mental model happen," and makes remote work-sample assessment **fraud-proof by construction** rather than by post-hoc triangulation.
- **Feasibility**: medium
- **Time-horizon**: quarters
- **Why it's a moonshot**: Every competitor (HackerRank, CodeSignal) either bans AI (now meaningless) or proctors with webcams (adversarial, hated). This product's existing thesis — *grade judgment in an AI-saturated world* — is the only framing where an **AI-allowed, process-observed** assessment makes sense. Owning the work surface turns the seed repo from a downloaded zip into a live instrument, and the `ProcessTrace`/`ToolingSignal` types stop being inferred and start being recorded ground truth. No incumbent can follow without abandoning their proctoring moat.
- **Path to implementation**:
  1. **STEP 1 (in scaffold)**: `pipeline/jobfit/devcase/seed_materializer.py` already produces the `{files:[{path,contents}]}` tree (≤12 files, auto-injected `DECISIONS.md`) that `SeedFiles.tsx` renders read-only. Add a `process_event` envelope type to `pipeline/jobfit/devcase/models.py` alongside `CommitReflection` (fields: `t`, `kind` ∈ open/edit/run/probe_touch/decision_log, `path`, `probeId?`) — the schema the workspace will emit, designed so `reflect.py` can consume an event stream OR a git log behind one interface.
  2. Embed a minimal in-browser editor over the materialized seed in `devcase/apply/[token]/page.tsx`, persisting the file tree + an append-only event log to a new `dev_session` table (mirror the `dev_submissions` row shape in `app/_lib/db/devcase.ts`).
  3. Add a scoped sandbox run (start with a single `npm test`-class command in a disposable container) so "ran the tests and they went red" becomes an observed event, not a claimed `verificationHabit`.
  4. Teach `reflect.py:assess_tooling` to prefer the observed event stream when present (deterministic, confidence ≥ 0.8) and fall back to the existing commit-metadata path (confidence ≤ 0.7) — preserving the propagated-MIN confidence invariant.
  5. Surface "observed vs. inferred" in `EvalPanel.tsx` next to the existing `ProvenanceStrip`, so a recruiter sees which signals were *watched* vs. *reconstructed*.
- **Dependencies**: a sandbox/container execution primitive (none exists today — `repo-snapshot.ts` only reads); the event schema from STEP 1; `dev-control.ts` audit must log session start/submit.
- **Risks**: scope/cost of a hosted execution sandbox; candidate hostility to "monitored" workspaces (mitigate: observe *process artifacts*, never keystrokes/screen — stay inside the existing fairness contract that bans inventing over-reliance from tool use); browser-IDE polish bar is high.
- **What changes if we ship it**: the product becomes the only remote assessment where you may use any AI and still be measured honestly — because we grade the judgment we *watched*, not the code we *assume*. Authenticity stops being a "suspect" band held for interview and becomes a recorded fact.

## 2. **The Adversarial Assessment Gym — a generative red-team that tries to beat every case before a human ever sees it**
- **Tier**: 1 (10x category-defining)
- **Category**: trust-layer
- **Impact**: For the hiring team's legal/credibility exposure and for the product's defensibility. Today `submission_eval.py` validates fairness against **6 authored personas** and `lifecycle_eval.py` plants 4 static defect kinds — a fixed, non-adaptive landscape. The 10x change: an autonomous **adversary agent** that, for any freshly designed `CaseScenario`, generates a *population* of attack submissions (the lazy AI-paster, the prompt-injector who tries to read `reveals`, the over-fitter who games the rubric, the genuinely-strong verifier) and runs them through the full `evaluate.py` pipeline. A case is not publishable until it **measurably discriminates** strong from gaming under attack — a generated "probe-strength under adversary" score that replaces the current static gate.
- **Feasibility**: medium
- **Time-horizon**: months
- **Why it's a moonshot**: It makes the assessment *falsifiable and self-hardening*. Every other assessment vendor ships static question banks that leak to coaching sites within weeks; here the same generative loop that designs the case also red-teams it, so leaked cases can be detected (the adversary finds the "known-answer" attack) and regenerated. It turns the existing hand-authored `BEHAVIORS` constant into a living, growing adversary corpus — a data-as-moat that compounds with every case ever designed.
- **Path to implementation**:
  1. **STEP 1 (in scaffold)**: `pipeline/jobfit/devcase/submission_scenarios.py` already defines `BEHAVIORS` and synthesizes ~48-row deterministic commit traces per persona. Add an `adversary` module beside it that, given a `CaseScenario`, asks the LLM-judge harness (`devcase/llm_judge.py:run_judge`) to *generate* a submission trace attempting to beat each `coverProbe` using its `decisionSpace` — emitting the same trace shape the existing personas use, so `submission_eval.py` consumes it unchanged.
  2. Add an `assess_probe_strength(case, adversary_results)` that scores each probe by how cleanly strong-beats-gaming under generated attack (extends the existing `discrimination` gate's `strong_beats_weak` margin to a *generated* cohort).
  3. Gate publication in `devcase-orchestrator.ts` at the `designed → approved` transition on a minimum probe-strength, routing weak cases back through `lifecycle/[id]/redesign` (the redesign path already exists).
  4. Persist adversary attempts to a `dev_adversary` table so leaked-case detection can flag a posting whose live submissions cluster suspiciously near a known successful attack.
  5. Surface the adversary verdict in `CaseDetail.tsx`'s existing internal "ProbeStrengthBanner" so reviewers see *why* a case is trusted.
- **Dependencies**: LLM-judge harness (exists); the orchestrator gate hook (exists at `gateApproval`); generation budget per case design.
- **Risks**: LLM cost of generating a population per case (mitigate: cache by case content-hash, run async/best-effort like the existing seed/scenario materialization); a weak adversary gives false confidence (mitigate: seed it with the existing authored personas as a floor).
- **What changes if we ship it**: "is this case any good?" stops being a designer's hope and becomes a measured, adversary-tested number — and the assessment hardens itself against the leakage that kills every static test bank.

## 3. **The Self-Tuning Autonomous Recruiter — a policy that learns from outcomes inside the fairness envelope, and narrates why**
- **Tier**: 1 (10x category-defining)
- **Category**: autonomous-agent
- **Impact**: For the recruiter and the buyer. Today autonomy is a *fixed* `POLICY` dict in `automation.py` and a *single human-applied* promote-floor from `dev-outcomes.py:calibrate()`. Nobody can tell whether the autonomous recruiter is good or merely confident, and every threshold (`bau_advance_score: 70`, `bau_reject_score: 40`, `screening_auto_days: 2`) is a hand-set guess. The 10x change: a closed loop that fits **every** policy threshold to measured downstream outcomes (advance→pass, promote→hire, the `dev_outcomes` band hire-rates), proposes bounded adjustments, A/B-shadows the new policy against the live one in **dry-run** (the `runAutomationPass({dryRun})` path already forecasts decisions without applying), and only promotes a change after it beats the incumbent on outcomes — every adjustment written to the immutable `dev_audit` trail with a human-readable rationale.
- **Feasibility**: medium
- **Time-horizon**: months
- **Why it's a moonshot**: It converts "trust our automation" into "here is its measured track record and the bounded, audited reason it changed its mind." The dry-run preview and the outcome store *already exist* — they have simply never been wired into a loop. Doing so makes the autonomous recruiter the first hiring agent that can prove it improves, inside a fairness envelope it can never escape (the early-career shield in `assertAutoRejectFair` stays a hard invariant the tuner cannot touch).
- **Path to implementation**:
  1. **STEP 1 (in scaffold)**: `app/_lib/dev-outcomes.ts:calibrate()` already joins `predicted_score` to real `hired/rejected` outcomes into bands and emits `{suggestedFloor, rationale, predictive}`. Generalize its band-fitting into a pure `proposePolicy(outcomes, currentPolicy)` that returns bounded deltas for *all* `POLICY` scalars (not just the floor), with the same MIN-SAMPLE and monotonicity guards — read-only, suggests nothing it can't justify.
  2. Add an outcome feed for the *general* pipeline mirroring `recordPipelineOutcome`, so advance/reject decisions get the same (prediction, outcome) joins the devcase path has.
  3. Run the proposed policy through the existing `runAutomationPass({dryRun:true})` against the live board nightly via `scheduler.ts`, recording a shadow-decision diff vs. the incumbent — no applies.
  4. Surface the proposal + shadow diff on `app/control/page.tsx` (which already renders calibration + an "Apply suggested floor" button) as an "Apply suggested policy" gate; never auto-apply policy changes (keep the human as the ratifier; the loop earns the recommendation).
  5. Write every applied change to `dev_audit` with actor `auto`/`human` and the fitted rationale, so the policy's whole evolution is replayable.
- **Dependencies**: outcome labels for the general pipeline (devcase already has them; general pipeline needs the feed); the dry-run path (exists); the control room UI (exists).
- **Risks**: feedback loops / drift (mitigate: hard bounds per scalar, the fairness invariants as untunable floors, shadow-before-promote); thin outcome data early (mitigate: the existing MIN_RESOLVED=4 guard — refuse to suggest below sample).
- **What changes if we ship it**: the autonomous recruiter stops being a set of guessed constants and becomes a measured, bounded, self-improving instrument whose every decision-rule change is audited and justified — the precondition for selling autonomy to a risk-averse enterprise.

## 4. **Apply-to-Assess Continuity — one LLM conversation that screens, sources the work sample, and starts the case in a single sitting**
- **Tier**: 2 (3-5x)
- **Category**: interface-expansion
- **Impact**: For candidates and for top-of-funnel conversion. Today `ConversationalApply.tsx` is a scripted stepper that ends by handing off a repo URL into the pipeline; the devcase apply (`devcase/apply/[token]`) is a *separate*, disconnected surface reached only after a recruiter sources and publishes. The 10x change: a single genuinely-LLM conversational surface where a candidate applies, the dialog *adaptively clarifies* skills (real follow-ups, not free-text), the engine scores fit live, and — for a strong-enough fit — the candidate is offered the materialized work-sample case **right there**, starting the Live Work Surface (Moonshot 1) in the same session. Apply and assess become one continuous candidate journey instead of two cold hand-offs.
- **Feasibility**: high
- **Time-horizon**: weeks
- **Why it's a moonshot (Tier 2)**: It is the connective tissue that makes Moonshots 1–3 land for the candidate — the difference between "we have an assessment product" and "applying *is* being assessed." It is genuinely conversational (the project has the multi-provider LLM layer already) where today it is a stepper, and it closes the apply→source→publish→assess gap that currently requires three recruiter actions. Lower tier only because it composes existing primitives rather than inventing a new one.
- **Path to implementation**:
  1. **STEP 1 (in scaffold)**: `app/_lib/apply.ts:buildApplyScript` builds the static step list and `apply-intake.ts:buildIntakeProfile` turns answers into a `CandidateProfileV2`. Add an `adaptive` step kind to the `ApplyStep` union whose prompt is generated from prior answers via the existing LLM layer — start with one targeted skill-deepening follow-up after the free-text skills step, behind a flag, falling back to the static script on any LLM failure (mirror the engine-wide `generate_with_fallback` contract).
  2. Compute a live fit signal after intake using the existing `match-candidate.ts` path and show a non-judgmental micro-preview in the chat.
  3. On a strong fit for a role that has a published devcase posting, offer the case inline and deep-link into `devcase/apply/[token]` (later: the embedded surface from Moonshot 1) carrying the just-built profile.
  4. Persist the apply↔devcase link so the eventual submission inherits the conversation's evidence (no re-keying).
- **Dependencies**: the multi-provider LLM layer (exists); Moonshot 1 for the in-session work surface (degrades gracefully to the existing token hand-off without it).
- **Risks**: LLM latency/cost in a public unauthenticated surface (mitigate: rate-limit via existing `rate-limit.ts`, cap turns, static fallback); over-clarifying annoys candidates (mitigate: one adaptive follow-up to start, measure completion).
- **What changes if we ship it**: applying becomes a continuous, intelligent conversation that can flow straight into doing the actual work — collapsing a multi-day, multi-tool funnel into one sitting and dramatically lifting qualified completion.

## 5. **The Portable, Verifier-Signed Competence Credential — a candidate-owned proof of what they actually did**
- **Tier**: 2 (3-5x)
- **Category**: new-market
- **Impact**: For candidates across the whole market, not just one hiring team. Today a `CaseEvaluation` + the observed process live only inside one recruiter's `dev_submissions` row; the candidate walks away with nothing portable, and every employer re-runs the same assessment from scratch. The 10x change: when a candidate completes a process-observed case (Moonshot 1), the platform mints a **candidate-owned, signed credential** — a tamper-evident artifact summarizing the rubric dimensions, the probe outcomes, and the *observed* process facts (not the raw work), that the candidate can present to *other* employers, who can cryptographically verify it was issued by this engine and not edited. This turns a single assessment into a reusable, candidate-controlled asset and seeds a cross-employer trust network.
- **Feasibility**: medium
- **Time-horizon**: quarters
- **Why it's a moonshot (Tier 2)**: It flips the unit of value from "the employer's private verdict" to "the candidate's portable proof," which is the only path to a network effect in hiring (more credentials → more employers accept them → more candidates want one). It leans entirely on assets the engine already produces honestly — provenance, propagated confidence, probe outcomes, the trust ledger — and packages them as a falsifiable, signed claim. Tier 2 because the network effect is the long game; the signed-artifact MVP is near-term.
- **Path to implementation**:
  1. **STEP 1 (in scaffold)**: `pipeline/jobfit/devcase/provenance.py` already stamps every artifact with a `confidence`, a tri-state `source` (`combine_source`), and a `fallbackReason`. Add a `credential.py` that serializes a `CaseEvaluation` + `ToolingSignal` + probe outcomes into a stable, minimal claim document (rubric dimension scores, probe handled/missed, observed-vs-inferred provenance) — deterministic, no new data, just a canonical projection of what already exists.
  2. Sign the claim server-side (HMAC/keypair) at promote time in `devcase-orchestrator.ts` and store the signature on the `dev_submissions` row.
  3. Expose a public token-gated verify page (mirror the `schedule/[token]`/`offer/[token]` pattern) that re-validates the signature and renders the claim without exposing the candidate's raw work.
  4. Give the candidate the credential link in the existing promote/feedback comms (`comms-dispatch.ts`).
  5. Add an inbound "present a credential" path on the apply surface (Moonshot 4) so a verified credential pre-fills high-provenance evidence — the first cross-employer reuse.
- **Dependencies**: Moonshot 1 for the *observed* facts that make the credential worth more than a self-claim; a signing key in `env.ts`; the public-token surface pattern (exists).
- **Risks**: privacy of what the credential reveals (mitigate: ship summary facts, never raw work; candidate opt-in); adoption chicken-and-egg across employers (mitigate: useful immediately as a candidate-owned record even before any second employer accepts it).
- **What changes if we ship it**: an assessment stops being a throwaway gate and becomes a durable, portable, verifier-signed asset the candidate owns — the seed of a hiring trust network the platform sits at the center of.
